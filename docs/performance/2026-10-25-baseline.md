# Cache performance and semantics baseline — Stage A

Frozen: 2026-10-25, evidence bundle `baseline-004` (re-collected after review
round 2 fixed two further P2 tooling defects; see Provenance). This report is
the authoritative Stage A baseline. Stage B/C candidates compare against
these numbers on the same machine, Go version, load, and CPU configuration.

## What this is and is not

Stage A only added measurement tooling and evidence. **No runtime policy
changed and no optimization is claimed.** The runtime files are byte-identical
to runtime baseline `12698ab9a095dcb130b5f60f8296950820f9c94b` (verified by
`git diff` and by the collection script before measuring). The Stage B goals
(foreground Set P99 −50%, ≤5% regression budget) and Stage C goals (≤10%
stats overhead) quoted in the design are **future acceptance targets, not
results achieved here**.

| `baseline-004` | Full re-collection on the round-2 fixes | **Authoritative.** 161 JSON records (all accounting equations verified; profiled runs excluded from every table), micro benchmarks ×10 across `-cpu=1,2,4,8`, lifecycle `resources_held`/`resources` two-phase JSON plus `-v` logs, profiles, manifest, SHA256SUMS |

## Micro benchmarks (ns/op, median of 10, GOMAXPROCS=8)

Every timed iteration contains exactly one cache operation (no harness
arithmetic). Uniform and zipf replays cycle over 2×capacity distinct keys;
scan replays one full sweep of a 4×capacity key pool, so the write half alone
is 2×capacity. Both premises are pinned by tests: `TestWorkloadFullTierEvicts`
(one cycle evicts most preloaded entries) and `TestWorkloadScanSustainsEviction`
(writes still miss in every post-warmup replay cycle — the superseded bundles
silently measured the overwrite path instead).

| Lookup hit | 23.5 | 294.8 | 0 | 0 |
| Lookup miss | 13.5 | 130.8 | 0 | 0 |
| Get hit | 28.8 | — | 8 | 1 (returned pointer) |
| Set overwrite | 61.6 | 419.2 | 0 | 0 |
| Insert with eviction | 112.5 | 350.6 | 55 | 0 |

Payload variants (hit Lookup / overwrite Set): int 21.4/58.5 ns; 256-byte
array copied by value 37.0/72.5 ns; shared pre-allocated 4 KiB slice

allocs/op; the only per-call allocation is Get's returned pointer.

### First post-expiry operation (one operation per fresh fixture)

| Capacity | FirstSet | FirstLookup | Size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 024 | 72.3 µs | 458 ns | 70.4 µs |
| 100 000 | 6.06 ms | 1.5 µs | 5.73 ms |
| 1 000 000 | 162.8 ms | 2.4 µs | 158.5 ms |

These one-operation-per-fixture cells are single cold-cache iterations:
within this bundle the ten repetitions spread ±2% (1K), ±13% (100K), and ±7%
(1M) — but the same cells moved up to ~68% between full collections under
different machine states (see Measurement stability). Their medians still
separate FirstLookup from FirstSet/Size by three to four orders of magnitude,
which is the signal Stage B compares.

The current `set` purges **every** due entry before writing, and `Size`
purges before counting: both scale linearly with the number of expired
entries. A Lookup that finds its own key expired removes only that key.
This is the central cost Stage B (B1) intends to bound; these numbers are
the un-optimized control.

Notes:

- The burst-window p99 of expiry-burst/size-burst (11–22 ms) is the
  first-op purge of 100 000 entries stalling everything behind the lock —
  two to three orders of magnitude above the steady p99 at the same rate.
  Spread deadlines cut the burst p99 at 10 k/50 k to 0.77/2.0 ms.
- **Queue-boundary sensitivity, now observed in-bundle:** this collection's
  purge stalls ran longer than `baseline-003`'s (16–22 ms vs 12–15 ms), and
  at 50 k rps the backlog crossed the 1024-slot queue: expiry-burst dropped
  567 requests across 10 runs (0 in six runs, 10–277 in four), size-burst
  1 613 (0 in one run, 6–278 in nine). Drops are counted, never hidden from
  the equations; Stage B comparisons must report the per-run drop count, not
  assume either regime.
- Set p99 sample counts at rate 1000 (150) are **low-sample**: those rows
  are kept raw but must not serve as percentage-acceptance tiers.
- steady reads draw uniform-random over a 2 048-key pool with half
  preloaded: the recorded hit rate is 48.7–51.3%, the expected value for the
  distribution.
- The rate-1000 tier's p50 (0.14–0.23 ms) sits at the generator floor (noop
  p50 0.224 ms): read it as scheduling quantization, not cache cost.

## Instance, worker, and payload costs

Idle observation: 1 rps no-op load (its own window is 2 s by construction —
the last request is planned at t=2 s), while the observation window is held
open the full 3 s before sampling. `empty_heap_checks` comes from an
instrumented `now()` (one atomic increment per call) — an honest report of
today's ticker behavior with its instrumentation overhead; Stage A makes no
"no periodic wake-up" claim.

| Instances | variant | extra goroutines | extra workers | heap after GC | now() calls / 3 s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | empty / future | 3 | 1 | ~208 KB | 30 / 31 |
| 100 | empty / future | 102 | 100 | 359–392 KB | 3 000 / 3 100 |
| 1 000 | empty / future | 1 002 | 1 000 | 1.71–2.04 MB | 30 000 / 31 000 |

Exactly 10 checks per instance per second: every TTL instance keeps one
cleaner goroutine waking on a 100 ms ticker even with an empty heap, and
retains ~1.7 KB. Proving "no periodic wake-up on an empty heap" is Stage
B2's acceptance work.

Lifecycle (10 rounds × 10 caches × 1024 entries, create/fill/Close; payload
references pinned by `runtime.KeepAlive` through the held sampling). Both
phases are durable JSON evidence — `resources_held` (payloads pinned) next
to `resources` (after release) — and the per-round trail is preserved by
running the lifecycle collections with `go test -v`:

| Value | total Close time (10 rounds) | registry delta | leaked workers | held heap | heap after release |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| int | 101 µs | 0 | 0 | — | 247 KB / 634 objects |
| 4 KiB payloads (102 400 slices) | 151 µs | 0 | 0 | **422 436 232 B / 103 125 objects** (≈403 MB) | 268 KB / 719 objects |

Registrations and workers do not grow across rounds; the ~403 MB held heap
collapses to metadata size once the payload references are dropped — the
caches hold no payload copies of their own.
`TestPerformanceRoundsReleaseOwnedCaches` additionally proves the checker
catches a deliberately leaked instance, and
`TestLifecycleScenarioRecordsPayloadBoundary` pins the two-phase record.

## Measurement stability

Splitting the 10-repetition micro benchmark file into halves and comparing
with benchstat keeps most deltas within ±1–2% (low-n halves flagged by
benchstat's marker). Between full collections on this machine the same
benchmarks move far more even though the runtime is byte-identical: the
50 k noop p99 read 13 µs (`baseline-001`), 68 µs (`003`), and 26 µs (`004`);
InsertEvict-100K read 356.9 ns (`003`) and 245.6 ns (`004`); FirstSet-1K
read 43.1 µs (`003`) and 72.3 µs (`004`). Absolute small-number comparisons
only make sense within one bundle — which is why each review round
re-collected everything at once. The expiry-burst burst p99 signal
(11–22 ms) sits two to three orders of magnitude above the floor in every
bundle.

## Limitations

- One machine, one Go version for performance numbers; no cross-machine
  claims. Ratios are the comparable currency, not absolute nanoseconds.
- Open-loop scenarios used the machine-default GOMAXPROCS (8); comparisons
  must match it (or use bench-full's explicit `-cpu` grid).
- Idle/empty-heap checks come from an instrumented run; the ticker count
  includes that instrumentation overhead.
- The purge-stall drop behavior crossed the 1024-queue boundary at 50 k rps
  in this bundle (two scenarios, per-run spread 0–278); drop counts must be
  reported per comparison run.
- Profiles were captured in separate single runs to locate costs, not to
  produce the tables above.

## How to replay

```sh
make perf-baseline PERFSUITE=full PERFOUT=/tmp/memcache-perf-<new>
```

then compare JSON records tier-by-tier (same case, rate, duration, workers,
queue) and micro benchmarks with benchstat. Never overwrite an evidence
directory; fresh paths only.
