# Cache performance and semantics baseline — Stage A

Frozen: 2026-10-25, evidence bundle `baseline-003` (re-collected after the
acceptance review fixed six P2 tooling defects; see Provenance). This report
is the authoritative Stage A baseline. Stage B/C candidates compare against
these numbers on the same machine, Go version, load, and CPU configuration.

## What this is and is not

Stage A only added measurement tooling and evidence. **No runtime policy
changed and no optimization is claimed.** The runtime files are byte-identical
to runtime baseline `12698ab9a095dcb130b5f60f8296950820f9c94b` (verified by
`git diff` and by the collection script before measuring). The Stage B goals
(foreground Set P99 −50%, ≤5% regression budget) and Stage C goals (≤10%
stats overhead) quoted in the design are **future acceptance targets, not
results achieved here**.

## Provenance

Three collections exist; only the third is authoritative:

| Bundle | What it was | Why superseded |
| --- | --- | --- |

## Micro benchmarks (ns/op, median of 10, GOMAXPROCS=8)

Every timed iteration contains exactly one cache operation (no harness
arithmetic). At large tiers the replay cycle holds 2×capacity distinct keys,
verified to keep evicting by `TestWorkloadFullTierEvicts` (uniform: ~50% of
writes miss the full cache; scan: every preloaded key evicted).

| Operation (1K tier) | sequential | parallel | B/op | allocs/op |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lookup hit | 24.2 | 298.8 | 0 | 0 |
| Lookup miss | 14.1 | 129 | 0 | 0 |
| Get hit | 30.3 | — | 8 | 1 (returned pointer) |
| Set overwrite | 64.6 | 425 | 0 | 0 |
| Insert with eviction | 122.2 | 379 | 55 | 0 |
| Uniform95 mix | 24.6 | 225 | 0 | 0 |
| Zipf95 mix | 25.9 | 217 | 0 | 0 |
| Scan50 mix | 93.7 | 278 | 54 | 0 |
| TTL renewal | 94.6 | 448 | 0 | 0 |

| Operation, sequential | cap 100K | cap 1M |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Insert with eviction | 356.9 | 468.1 |
| Uniform95 mix | 114.5 | 238.3 |
| Zipf95 mix | 47.6 | 85.0 |
| Scan50 mix | 78.4 | 177.7 |
| Lookup hit (whole-map working set) | — | 294.0 |

Payload variants (hit Lookup / overwrite Set): int 21.6/59.2 ns; 256-byte
array copied by value 36.5/72.2 ns; shared pre-allocated 4 KiB slice
(header-only store) 23.3/60.1 ns. Scalar hits and overwrites stay at 0
allocs/op; the only per-call allocation is Get's returned pointer.

### First post-expiry operation (one operation per fresh fixture)

| Capacity | FirstSet | FirstLookup | Size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 024 | 43 125 ns | 396 ns | 42 646 ns |
| 100 000 | 6.05 ms | 2 917 ns | 6.19 ms |
| 1 000 000 | 169.7 ms | 2 479 ns | 152.4 ms |

These one-operation-per-fixture cells are the noisiest in the bundle (single
cold-cache iterations; min–max spreads of ±30% across the ten repetitions —
see bench-expiry.log). Their medians still separate FirstLookup from
FirstSet/Size by three to four orders of magnitude, which is the signal
Stage B compares.

The current `set` purges **every** due entry before writing, and `Size`
purges before counting: both scale linearly with the number of expired
entries. A Lookup that finds its own key expired removes only that key.
This is the central cost Stage B (B1) intends to bound; these numbers are
the un-optimized control.

## Open-loop scenarios (3s window, 4 workers, queue 1024; median of 10)

Latency runs from the planned arrival to completion; `elapsed` covers the
load window (planned start → joins), so every record shows ~3 000 ms and
`completed/elapsed` is an honest throughput. All accounting equations
verified on all 161 records.

| noop | 50 000 | 5 µs | 68 µs | 43 µs | 6.0 ms | — | 0 |

Notes:

- The burst-window p99 of expiry-burst/size-burst (12–15 ms) is the
  first-op purge of 100 000 entries stalling everything behind the lock —
  two to three orders of magnitude above the steady p99 at the same rate.
  Spread deadlines cut that to 0.5–5.4 ms.
- **Queue-boundary sensitivity:** this collection recorded zero drops — the
  purge stalls (11–15 ms) at 50 k rps back up ~600–750 requests, inside the
  1024-slot queue. The superseded first collection, whose stalls ran
  ~20–24 ms under the same configuration, overflowed the queue (~130–250
  drops per run). Drops appear when stall × rate exceeds queue depth;
  Stage B comparisons must report the drop count per run, not assume
  either regime.
- Set p99 sample counts at rate 1000 (150) are **low-sample**: those rows
  are kept raw but must not serve as percentage-acceptance tiers.
- steady reads draw uniform-random over a 2 048-key pool with half
  preloaded: the recorded hit rate is 49–51%, the expected value for the
  distribution (the superseded collection's 54–64% reflected the sequential
  walk defect).
- The rate-1000 tier's p50 (0.13 ms) sits at the generator floor (noop p50
  0.129 ms): read it as scheduling quantization, not cache cost.

| Scenario | median reclaim | min–max |
| --- | --- | --- |
| expiry-burst @ 1 k | 23.6 ms | 20.8–32.3 ms |
| expiry-burst @ 10 k | 20.4 ms | 16.9–25.9 ms |
| expiry-burst @ 50 k | 11.5 ms | 9.3–15.0 ms |

Burst reclamation completes in tens of milliseconds (the first write/Size
purges everything at once); spread reclamation ends right after the last
deadline passes. Stage B's "reclaim ≤ 2× baseline" comparisons use these.

## Instance, worker, and payload costs

Idle observation: 1 rps no-op load (its own window is 2 s by construction —
the last request is planned at t=2 s), while the observation window is held
open the full 3 s before sampling. `empty_heap_checks` comes from an
instrumented `now()` (one atomic increment per call) — an honest report of
today's ticker behavior with its instrumentation overhead; Stage A makes no
"no periodic wake-up" claim.

| Instances | variant | extra goroutines | extra workers | heap after GC | now() calls / 3 s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | empty / future | 3 | 1 | ~213 KB | 30 / 31 |
| 100 | empty / future | 102 | 100 | 376–396 KB | 3 000 / 3 100 |
| 1 000 | empty / future | 1 002 | 1 000 | 1.72–2.04 MB | 30 000 / 31 000 |

Exactly 10 checks per instance per second: every TTL instance keeps one
cleaner goroutine waking on a 100 ms ticker even with an empty heap, and
retains ~1.7 KB. Proving "no periodic wake-up on an empty heap" is Stage
B2's acceptance work.

Lifecycle (10 rounds × 10 caches × 1024 entries, create/fill/Close; payload
references pinned by `runtime.KeepAlive` through the held sampling):

| Value | total Close time (10 rounds) | registry delta | leaked workers | heap after final GC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| int | 113 µs | 0 | 0 | 243 KB / 646 objects |
| 4 KiB payloads (102 400 slices ≈ 400 MB held during rounds) | 155 µs | 0 | 0 | 270 KB / 715 objects after release |

Registrations and workers do not grow across rounds; after releasing the
payloads the heap returns to metadata size. The held/released boundary
numbers are in the collection logs (`perf-lifecycle-bytes4k.log`).
`TestPerformanceRoundsReleaseOwnedCaches` additionally proves the checker
catches a deliberately leaked instance.

## Measurement stability

Splitting the 10-repetition micro benchmark file into halves and comparing
with benchstat keeps most deltas within ±1–2% (low-n halves flagged by
benchstat's marker). Between full collections on this machine the noop
floor itself moves (e.g. the 50 k noop p99 read 13 µs in the superseded
bundle and 68 µs in this one under different thermal/background states) —
absolute small-number comparisons only make sense within one bundle, which
is why baseline-003 supersedes everything at once. The expiry-burst burst
p99 signal (12–15 ms) sits two to three orders of magnitude above the floor
in either state.

## Limitations

- One machine, one Go version for performance numbers; no cross-machine
  claims. Ratios are the comparable currency, not absolute nanoseconds.
- Open-loop scenarios used the machine-default GOMAXPROCS (8); comparisons
  must match it (or use bench-full's explicit `-cpu` grid).
- Idle/empty-heap checks come from an instrumented run; the ticker count
  includes that instrumentation overhead.
- The purge-stall drop behavior sits near the 1024-queue boundary at
  50 k rps; drop counts must be reported per comparison run.
- Profiles were captured in separate single runs to locate costs, not to
  produce the tables above.

## How to replay

```sh
make perf-baseline PERFSUITE=full PERFOUT=/tmp/memcache-perf-<new>
```

then compare JSON records tier-by-tier (same case, rate, duration, workers,
queue) and micro benchmarks with benchstat. Never overwrite an evidence
directory; fresh paths only.
