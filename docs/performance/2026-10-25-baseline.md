# Cache performance and semantics baseline — Stage A

Frozen: 2026-10-25 (actual collection date; UTC 09:34–10:26). This report is
the authoritative Stage A baseline. Stage B/C candidates compare against
these numbers on the same machine, Go version, load, and CPU configuration.

## What this is and is not

Stage A only added measurement tooling and evidence. **No runtime policy
changed and no optimization is claimed.** The runtime files are byte-identical
to runtime baseline `12698ab9a095dcb130b5f60f8296950820f9c94b` (verified by
`git diff` and by the collection script before measuring); the collection ran
at git HEAD `3d6b159cc60dd2a0f404a9a73d1342daef7624ad` (test-tooling commits
only). The Stage B goals (foreground Set P99 −50%, ≤5% regression budget) and
Stage C goals (≤10% stats overhead) quoted in the design are **future
acceptance targets, not results achieved here**.

### First post-expiry operation (one operation per fresh fixture)

| Capacity | FirstSet | FirstLookup | Size |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 024 | 82 792 ns | 541 ns | 63 438 ns |
| 100 000 | 5.56 ms | 1 396 ns | 5.70 ms |
| 1 000 000 | 144.8 ms | 2 063 ns | 145.0 ms |

The current `set` purges **every** due entry before writing, and `Size`
purges before counting: both scale linearly with the number of expired
entries. A Lookup that finds its own key expired removes only that key.
This is the central cost Stage B (B1) intends to bound; these numbers are
the un-optimized control.

> Measurement note: these frozen cells were timed with the clock advance
> (~2–5 ns, a `time.Time` addition) still inside the timed region; the
> audit moved the advance outside the timer on the branch. The bias is
> below the run-to-run noise of every cell and matters only when Stage B
> reuses this benchmark for acceptance — it should compare against a
> re-collected baseline with the corrected boundary.

Notes:

- The burst-window p99 of expiry-burst/size-burst (15–23 ms) is the
  first-op purge of 100 000 entries stalling everything behind the lock —
  three orders of magnitude above the steady p99 at the same rate. Spread
  deadlines cut that to 1.3–3.4 ms at 10–50 k rps.
- At 50 k rps the purge stall also overflows the 1024-slot queue: ~130
  (expiry-burst) and ~249 (size-burst) dropped requests per run, vs 0 for
  steady and spread. Drops are counted, never hidden.
- Set p99 sample counts at rate 1000 (150) are **low-sample**: those rows
  are kept raw but must not serve as percentage-acceptance tiers.
- The rate-1000 tier's p50 (0.22–0.23 ms) sits at the generator floor
  (noop p50 0.223 ms): interpret that tier's p50 as scheduling quantization,
  not cache cost. Its p99/burst columns still carry real signal.
- Reads in expiry scenarios always hit the 1024 permanent keys (100%);
  steady shows the natural ~53–64% mixed hit rate of a half-loaded cache.

### Reclamation of the 100 000 expired entries (monitor runs, pure snapshots)

| Scenario | median reclaim | min–max |
| --- | --- | --- |
| expiry-burst @ 10 k | 17.6 ms | 17.2–24.6 ms |
| expiry-burst @ 1 k | 23.7 ms | 21.3–28.5 ms |
| expiry-burst @ 50 k | 11.3 ms | 10.4–17.4 ms |
| expiry-spread (all rates) | ~1 005–1 015 ms | equals the 1 s deadline window plus a tick |

Burst reclamation completes in tens of milliseconds (the first write/Size
purges everything at once); spread reclamation ends right after the last
deadline passes. Stage B's "reclaim ≤ 2× baseline" comparisons use these.

## Instance, worker, and payload costs

Idle observation (3 s, capacity-1 TTL instances, ticker reality of today's
cleaner — instrumented `now()` run, one atomic increment per call):

| Instances | variant | extra goroutines | extra workers | heap after GC | now() calls in 3 s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | empty / future | 3 | 1 | ~208 KB | 20 / 21 |
| 100 | empty / future | 102 | 100 | 369–390 KB | 2 000 / 2 100 |
| 1 000 | empty / future | 1 002 | 1 000 | 1.70–2.03 MB | 20 000 / 21 000 |

Every TTL instance keeps one cleaner goroutine waking ~10×/s (100 ms
ticker) even with an empty heap — ~20 now() calls per instance per 3 s and
~1.7 KB retained per instance. Stage A reports this as-is; **proving "no
periodic wake-up on an empty heap" is Stage B2's acceptance work.**

Lifecycle (10 rounds × 10 caches × 1024 entries, create/fill/Close):

| Value | total Close time (10 rounds) | registry delta | leaked workers | heap after final GC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| int | 106 µs | 0 | 0 | 250 KB / 646 objects |
| 4 KiB payloads (102 400 slices ≈ 400 MB retained during rounds) | 125 µs | 0 | 0 | 269 KB / 715 objects after payload release |

Registrations and workers do not grow across rounds; after releasing the
payload references the heap returns to metadata size — the payload/metadata
boundary is clean. `TestPerformanceRoundsReleaseOwnedCaches` additionally
proves the checker catches a deliberately leaked instance.

## Measurement stability

Splitting the 10-repetition micro benchmark file into two halves and
comparing with benchstat shows most deltas within ±1% (worst observed
~5% on one low-variance cell with n=5 halves, flagged by benchstat's
low-sample marker). The full-bench halves and commands are reproducible via
`make bench-full` on the same machine. Run-to-run scenario medians above
varied by a few percent; the expiry-burst burst p99 varied ~15.6–23 ms —
an order-of-magnitude effect, far above noise.

## Limitations

- One machine, one Go version for performance numbers; no cross-machine
  claims. Absolute nanoseconds are hardware-specific; ratios are the
  comparable currency.
- Open-loop scenarios used the machine-default GOMAXPROCS (8); comparisons
  must match it (or use bench-full's explicit `-cpu` grid).
- Idle/empty-heap checks come from an instrumented run; the ticker count
  includes that instrumentation overhead.
- No CPU-time accounting was taken during idle scenarios; process CPU is
  background observation only.
- Profiles were captured in separate single runs to locate costs, not to
  produce the tables above.
