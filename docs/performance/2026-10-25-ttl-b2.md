# Stage B2 acceptance — earliest-deadline scheduling and bounded batches

Executed 2026-10-26 on the development branch. This report closes Stage B:
it states the A→B2 gate result, the B1→B2 attribution, and the items that
remain open. It does not retroactively change the Stage A baseline or the B1
intermediate report.

## Method

- `scripts/perf-compare.sh --suite full`, one shared harness, ten interleaved
  control/candidate pairs with AB/BA order rotation, one subprocess per sample,
  never concurrently. Micro benchmarks ran at `-cpu=1,2,4,8`; open-loop runs
  used the machine default GOMAXPROCS (8), 4 workers, queue 1024, 3s windows,
  250ms burst window, seed 20261025. Idle and lifecycle ran one pair per side.
- Go 1.27.0 darwin/arm64, Apple M1 Pro (8 cores).
- Every load run passed the harness's `verifyLoadAccounting` equations; no run
  reported a nonzero exit.
- `cand/ctrl paired` is the median of the ten candidate/control ratios; below 1
  means the candidate was faster. Percent reduction is `1 − ratio`.

Twenty-pair re-sample: `BenchmarkCache/Lookup` 1.053 at cpu=1 (ratio range
1.016–1.081) and 1.056 at cpu=8 (0.971–1.134); `Set` 1.006 and 0.999;

**Scalar Lookup is about 1.2 ns slower (≈5.3–5.6% in the larger sample), just
over the 5% bound.** The non-TTL path gained one nil-channel guard; the size is
consistent with code layout plus that guard rather than a new hot-path
allocation. It is reported as a marginal gate miss, not a pass: more samples
made the regression clearer, not smaller.

`RenewTTL` — a TTL operation, so outside the "non-TTL" gate — regresses 10% at
cpu=1 and about 50% at cpu=2/4/8 in the synthetic loop that renews the current
earliest deadline on every iteration. That loop changes the heap root every
time, so the worker stops and resets its timer on every renewal. Mixed renewal
workloads, where the root rarely changes, stay within 4%
(`BenchmarkCacheWorkload/RenewTTL-cap1024` 1.009–1.043 across CPU counts). This
is the scheduler's inherent cost on a pathological pattern and an input for the
design review below; it is not hidden or counted as a pass.

The 1M-capacity mixed workloads remain noisy on this machine; they were not
used for a 5% verdict in the B1 report either.

## B1 → B2 attribution

The second comparison uses B1 as the control and runs the identical harness and
matrix (comparison role `incremental`, so it is attribution only, never a Stage
B verdict). It shows that the remaining B1 stall was exactly the old 100ms
full-purge cleaner, and what the scheduler change costs.

| expiry-burst 10K `burst_set.p99` | 7.666ms | 0.078ms | 0.012 | −98.8% |
| expiry-burst 50K `burst_set.p99` | 8.864ms | 0.107ms | 0.011 | −98.9% |
| expiry-burst 10K reclaim | 23.1ms | 26.1ms | 1.155 | B2 16% slower |
| expiry-burst 50K reclaim | 22.3ms | 29.0ms | 1.124 | B2 12% slower |
| expiry-spread 50K `set.p99` | 0.253ms | 0.051ms | 0.266 | B2 3.8× faster |
| idle-empty n=1000 `empty_heap_checks` | 30,000 | 1,000 | — | −97% |
| idle-empty n=1000 `tail_now_checks` | 10,309 | 0 | — | periodic wake gone |
| `BenchmarkCache/Lookup` (cpu 1–8) | ≈21.4ns | ≈22.5ns | 1.048–1.056 | B2 ≈5% slower |
| `BenchmarkCache/RenewTTL` cpu=1 | 168.4ns | 186.1ns | 1.109 | B2 11% slower |

Attribution: B1 already removed the foreground purge; what B2 removes is the
ticker's full purge, which is why the burst Set P99 falls another ~99% relative
to B1 while drops stay at zero. B2 also reverses B1's spread-scenario
regression. In exchange, total reclamation is 12–16% slower than B1 (batches of
64 instead of one purge), and the wake/re-arm path costs scalar Lookup about
1.2ns and the pathological self-root-renewal loop the values above. The 1K tier
is low-signal in both comparisons and is not used.

**Stage B is not declared a clean pass.** The headline latency goal is met by a
wide margin and nothing regressed in drops, throughput, or allocations, but two
items miss their bounds: scalar Lookup is ~5.3–5.6% slower, and full
reclamation at expiry-burst 50K takes 2.17–2.35× the control's time. Both are
inputs to the design review the plan requires; neither was fixed by silently
changing the batch size or removing samples.

## Design-review inputs and unresolved items

1. **Reclamation wall time (2.17–2.35× at 50K).** B2 trades a single long purge
   for 1,563 batches of 64, which is why per-request P99 collapses but the
   complete reclamation takes longer. The harness monitor samples about every
   10ms, so a 14ms vs 32ms comparison has coarse resolution; the plan requires a
   design review above 2×, and a larger batch (or a different yield policy) is a
   design revision, not a silent test change.
2. **Scalar Lookup +1.2ns (~5.3–5.6%).** The non-TTL path gained one nil-channel
   guard; the cost is just over the 5% bound and was confirmed with 20 pairs.
3. **RenewTTL on a self-root-renewal loop (10% at cpu=1, ~50% at cpu≥2).** Every
   renewal moves the earliest deadline, so the worker re-arms its timer. Mixed
   renewal workloads stay within 4%. A design revision could skip wakes for
   later-only root changes, but that would also change the required
   re-read-on-root-deletion behavior, so it belongs in the review.
4. **1K rate tier** has only ten burst Set samples and no signal above noop; it
   stays reported but excluded from percentage acceptance.
5. **Linux/Windows matrix** was not executed here. The Go 1.18 gate ran on
   darwin/arm64 because that is the only old toolchain present.
6. `GODEBUG=asynctimerchan=1` is unavailable on Go 1.27; the real Go 1.18 run
   is the compatibility evidence.

## Limitations

- One machine/OS and one CPU model; the usual benchmark caveats apply.
- Idle and lifecycle scenarios have one sample per side; they are resource
  observations, not percentage verdicts.
- The reclamation metric depends on the harness's 10ms monitor snapshots near
  the 2× boundary.
- Raw evidence stays outside the repository; the manifests and checksums are
  the durable record of what ran.
- This report does not rewrite the Stage A baseline or the B1 report, and it
  does not claim results for platforms or toolchains it did not run.
